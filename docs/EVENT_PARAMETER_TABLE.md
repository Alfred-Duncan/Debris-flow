# 2026-08-26 Jilong debris-flow event: event-parameter evidence table

## Scope and reading rules

This is an evidence inventory for the model stage **after the debris flow entered the main river and before/at Jilong Port**.  It is not a source prescription, hydrograph, calibration target set, or a D-Claw configuration.  In particular, a reach-average travel speed or a port peak-discharge estimate must not be re-labelled as a main-river-entry condition.

The terms “source”, “entry”, and “port” distinguish the reported high-elevation ice-rock-avalanche initiation area, its as-yet-unlocated entry to the main river, and Jilong Port respectively.  Reported figures sometimes cover the complete avalanche--debris-flow chain rather than the downstream main-river reach.

Evidence classes used in every table row:

- **A. DIRECT / OFFICIAL OBSERVATION** — mapped, measured, or officially reported observation.  It may still have mapping uncertainty.
- **B. PEER-REVIEWED RECONSTRUCTION / ESTIMATE** — a reconstruction/estimate reported with the 2026 *Science Bulletin* study or its official Institute of Tibetan Plateau Research summary.
- **C. SECONDARY ESTIMATE** — preliminary technical estimate, expert statement, or secondary report without a public reproducible method/data package.
- **D. UNKNOWN / NOT PUBLICLY AVAILABLE** — no quantitative public value was located in the priority sources reviewed as of 2026-09-14.

## Source-condition implications

The evidence supports a clear process narrative: an approximately 5,200 m high ice-rock avalanche descended approximately 3,400 m along an approximately 22 km path, mixed with water and sediment near approximately 4,000 m, and underwent progressive entrainment.  For the **post-main-river-entry** stage, the only quantitative downstream constraints found are a preliminary approximately 15 km travel distance, approximately 30 min travel time, approximately 8.3 m s^-1 reach-average speed, and a Jilong Port peak-discharge estimate of approximately 3–5 × 10^4 m^3 s^-1.  They are not a complete source condition.

1. **Do we know the complete upstream inflow hydrograph Q(t)?** No.  No published time series, duration, rising/falling limbs, or entry-section discharge record was located.
2. **Do we know entry velocity?** No.  The approximately 8.3 m s^-1 figure is a bulk downstream reach average; the approximately 50 m s^-1 figure is a secondary whole-chain high-speed statement.  Neither is an entry velocity.
3. **Do we know entry flow depth?** No.  No event-time depth, surveyed high-water mark tied to a section, or entry cross-section was located.
4. **Do we know entry solid fraction?** No.  Public sources describe entrainment and water-rich material qualitatively, without a numerical composition.
5. **Do we know source volume at the main-river entry?** No.  The public “over 30 million m^3” statement has an unresolved material/volume definition and does not identify the entry-stage volume.
6. **Which quantities are directly known?** Officially reported direct observations include the approximately 1.2 × 10^6 m^2 source/near-source scour area, approximately 8.4 × 10^6 m^2 downstream scour area, approximately 0.7 km^2 affected core area, and post-event residual river discharges.  The key path/elevation/time values are reconstructions or rapid estimates, not direct entry measurements.
7. **Which quantities must still be inferred/calibrated?** The entry location and geometry; Q(t); start time and duration; depth; velocity vector; mixture/solid fraction; entry-stage volume; density and grain size; basal/rheological parameters; pore-pressure state; and erosion/entrainment parameters all remain to be independently inferred or calibrated.  None should be filled with defaults on the basis of this table.
